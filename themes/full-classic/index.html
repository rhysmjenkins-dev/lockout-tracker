App guide

How to use the Lockout Tracker

This app records hand-by-hand scores and generates detailed statistics.

On this page OverviewSign in and accessStart a sessionScore handsLate joinersProfiles and statsELO ratingsData and privacy

Overview

Everything needed to record a game

The tracker records sessions, hand-by-hand scores, lockout attempts and player performance. Players enter each hand’s scores and choose who declared lockout; the app handles successful and false-lockout calculations automatically.

Member access

Sign in with your player PIN

  1. Select Sign in to edit or the identity control in the header.
  2. Choose your player name.
  3. Enter your personal four-digit PIN.

Your signed-in identity is remembered on that device until you sign out. Signed-in friends can create and score sessions; a player can edit only their own profile.

Starting a game

Create a session

  1. Add a session title and choose the host.
  2. Add optional notes and tags such as Casual, Holiday, Pub or Tournament.
  3. Set the false-lockout penalty.
  4. Select at least two players and add an optional session photo.
  5. Press Start session.
Testing sessions stay visible in history but are excluded from official statistics, profiles, comparisons, achievements and ELO.

During play

Record each hand

  1. Enter every player’s score.
  2. Select who declared lockout, or choose No lockout.
  3. When someone locked out, input their actual lockout score—the score they called lockout with.
  4. Add an optional note and submit the hand.

The tracker determines whether the lockout was successful. Successful lockouts score 0 unless the player’s actual total was negative. False lockouts score the declared total plus the session penalty.

Mid-session

Add a late joiner

Use Add late joiner in the active session. Their starting score is the current players’ average cumulative score, rounded to the nearest whole number. Their tables and graph labels show the hand at which they joined, for example Russ (H2).

After and between games

Profiles, sessions and statistics

  • Players contains bios, ELO history, achievements, head-to-head records and recent sessions.
  • Previous sessions contains final scores, Worm and Manhattan graphs, and full hand history.
  • Statistics contains overall tables, head-to-head records, detailed comparisons and ELO rankings.
  • Avg lockout score is the mean score at which a player declared lockout, including both successful and false attempts.

Skill rating

How ELO works

All players start at 1000. The current tracker uses finishing position rather than raw score: a player gains rating for finishing above expectation and loses rating for finishing below expectation.

  • Provisional players with fewer than 50 hands are marked with ? and use K=40, so their rating finds its level faster.
  • Established players use K=24 for more stable movement.
  • Different K-factors and whole-number rounding mean the displayed changes may not add up to exactly zero.
  • Testing sessions do not affect ELO.

Data and privacy

A trusted group with clear boundaries

  • Anyone can view public scores and statistics.
  • A player name and four-digit PIN are required before making changes.
  • Each player can edit only their own profile.
  • Edit history records who changed sessions and hands, and when.
  • The administrator can manage data and access through the connected Google Sheet and Apps Script project.

Glossary of terms

Game and app reference

Definitions used in the rules, score entry and statistics.

Lockout

Declaring that you have 5 points or less at the start of your turn. If successful—strictly lowest score—you score 0 for the hand.

False Lockout

A failed attempt where your score is greater than 5, or tied with or higher than another player. Your scored hand is the lockout score plus the session penalty.

Lockout Score

The actual hand total when a player declared lockout. It is stored separately for statistics, even when a successful attempt scores 0.

Hand

A single round of play, from deal to lockout reveal. Multiple hands make up a session.

Session

A complete game consisting of multiple hands.

Formation

The arrangement of face-down cards, typically a 2×2 grid or 1×4 row. Cards stay in their original positions for memory tracking.

Magic Cards

Drawn cards with abilities: 7 looks at your own card, 8 at an opponent’s, 9 is a blind swap, 10 an open swap, and a Red Jack swaps between two opponents.

Slam

An out-of-turn action matching a discarded card with one in your hand or an opponent’s hand.

Late Joiner

A player who joins mid-session. Their starting score is the existing players’ average cumulative score, rounded to the nearest whole number.

Worm Chart

A line graph showing cumulative scores over time.

Manhattan Chart

A bar chart showing hand-by-hand scores.

Head-to-Head Record

Direct win, draw and loss record between two players when they appear in the same completed official session.

Average Lockout Score

The mean score at which a player declared lockout, including successful and false attempts. Lower is better.

Win Rate

Sessions won divided by sessions played. A tied session shares one win equally between its tied winners.

Session Tags

Categories such as Casual, Holiday, Pub and Tournament. Testing sessions remain in history but are excluded from official statistics, profiles, comparisons, achievements and ELO.

Player Sign-In

Select your name and enter your four-digit PIN. This identifies who is making changes and is remembered on that device until sign-out.

Edit History

A log of changes to sessions and hands, tracking who changed what and when.

ELO Rating

A skill rating that starts at 1000 and moves when a player finishes above or below expectation.

Provisional Rating (?)

A rating for a player with fewer than 50 hands. It moves faster to help find the player’s level.

K-Factor

Controls the size of an ELO change. Provisional players use K=40; established players use K=24.

Rank-Based ELO

ELO calculated from finishing position rather than raw score. It supports any number of players.

Game reference

Lockout rules

Be the player with the lowest cumulative score at the end of the session.

01

Set up

  • Use one standard 52-card deck; no jokers.
  • Deal 4 cards face down to every player.
  • Arrange cards in a formation, commonly 2×2 or 1×4.
  • Look at any 2 of your own cards once, then return them face down in the same positions.
02

Card values

−1Red King0Black King1Ace2–10Number cards10Jack / Queen
03

Taking a turn

Draw from the deck or discard pile. Either swap the drawn card into your formation and discard the replaced card, or discard the drawn card. Cards stay in position unless removed by a slam.

04

Magic cards

  • 7: look at one of your own cards.
  • 8: look at one opponent’s card.
  • 9: blindly swap one of your cards with an opponent’s.
  • 10: look at both cards, then choose whether to swap.
  • Red Jack: swap cards between two opponents.

The ability applies only when the card is drawn from the deck and immediately discarded.

05

Slams

Match the top discard with a card in your own or another player’s formation. A correct own slam removes your card. A correct opponent slam gives them a face-down penalty card. Incorrect slams give the slammer a penalty card.

  • Suits do not matter.
  • Red Kings cannot be slammed.
  • You cannot slam the card you just discarded.
  • You may defend your card by touching it first.
06

Lockout

Declare “Lockout” only at the start of your turn, before drawing, when your hand total is 5 or less. Your hand freezes and every other player takes one final turn.

Successful lockout

You are strictly lower than every other player. Score 0, unless your actual total is negative—in that case keep the negative score.

False lockout

Your total is above 5, tied with, or higher than another player. Score your actual hand total plus the agreed penalty.

07

Winning the session

Add scores across every hand. The player with the lowest cumulative score wins. The group can choose when to finish: after a fixed number of hands, at a score target or at the end of the event.

Good luck, and may the lowest score win.
